The client resolves the hostname again when making the HTTP request (unless the OS caches it perfectly, but even then, the protection relies on the assumption that the IP won't change).

An attacker could set up a domain that resolves to a public IP initially (to pass the check) and then switch to an internal IP just before the server makes the request, or rely on the fact that the validation happened on a snapshot of the DNS state.
